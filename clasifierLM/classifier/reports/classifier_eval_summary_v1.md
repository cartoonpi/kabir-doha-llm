**Which model wins.** Both learned models clearly beat the keyword list on themes (human macro-F1 0.63–0.64 vs 0.55), and the fastText-style model meets every acceptance target. But it does **not** beat logistic regression: on the human set logreg is slightly ahead (top-1 0.714 vs 0.679, macro-F1 0.643 vs 0.629, image micro-F1 0.857 vs 0.800). With 56 human requests, one request is worth 1.8 points, so this is a tie within noise. The fastText-style model's real advantage is practical: it handles unseen words through character 3-grams and exports to a dependency-free browser module. All models drop about 15 points from synthetic test to human requests (fastText top-1 0.817 → 0.679). That gap, caused by training only on one LLM's writing style, is the biggest problem.

**Where it fails.** (1) *A "death" magnet*: melancholy but non-death words (*dry and broken*, *silence*, *old memories*, *anger*) get pulled into `mrityu` with high confidence (errors 1, 2, 4, 6). (2) *Image words imply a theme*: in training, "none" requests never mention lamps, fish or markets, so *"why is the fish jumping out of water in this pond"* becomes `maya` (0.78). (3) *Overconfidence on short, unfamiliar text*: *"make a panini"* gives `satsang` (0.72) plus two images at about 0.9. A 3-word request is the average of only about 10 piece vectors, so a few hash collisions can dominate. (4) Neighbouring themes blur (`bhakti` / `prem` / `pakhand`), sometimes defensibly (*"true devotion comes from within not outside rituals"*).

**What to try next.** Add synthetic "none" requests that mention image objects in everyday contexts (fish tanks, grocery markets, boat rides), plus short off-topic requests. Diversify the writers, for example by mixing local-LLM (Qwen) data with the Sonnet data, to shrink the synthetic-to-human gap. Write 30–50 more human requests, because 56 is too few to separate models that are this close. Try word-dropout augmentation and lower `t_theme` alternatives once a bigger human set exists to check them against. Consider shipping logreg-equivalent weights if the tie persists.
